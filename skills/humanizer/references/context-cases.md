# Context cases: role and genre failures

Use the case that matches the communication problem. These cases come from the
user's earlier research and the v2 draft of this skill. They are generic editorial
illustrations, not measured results or approved wording. Keep the actual user's facts
and purpose; never import facts from an example.

## 1. An insider made to sound like an external auditor

**Context:** An engineer has investigated slow build jobs and wants to discuss
prioritizing the fix with a direct technical manager familiar with the system.
There is no stated uncertainty about organizational ownership.

**Mismatch:** "Who owns resource allocation for the build agents?"

The sentence asks the manager to explain an ownership question instead of addressing
the known technical problem. Replacing "resource allocation" with "build configuration"
does not fix that change of role or the unsupported ownership question.

**Possible repair:** "The current build configuration is causing delays. I want
to discuss whether to prioritize fixing it."

**Boundary:** When responsibility really is unclear between two teams, a question
such as "Which team will handle the migration?" may be appropriate. Do not suppress
that genuine uncertainty or invent an existing owner.

## 2. Personal talking points turned into a facilitated session

**Context:** Someone asks for notes to help them talk to a colleague. The only timing
information is that they have 30 minutes available.

**Mismatch:** "17–25 min: Remediation decisions."

The writer invented a running order. Reducing the table width, changing the time
notation, or replacing the range with "8 minutes" leaves the same wrong format.

**Possible repair:** "Options for fixing this and which one to try first."

**Boundary:** A requested timed workshop agenda may need time allocations. Label
newly proposed timings as proposed, not as an established schedule. Do not remove
a real schedule just because the same formatting was wrong for personal notes.

## 3. A request for support turned into managerial authority

**Context:** An individual contributor wants to ask a manager for capacity and
prioritization. No assignments or delivery commitments have been agreed.

**Mismatch:** "Assign accountable owners and deliverables."

This is a management instruction, not the contributor's actual request. It can
also invent a responsibility gap that the source never established.

**Possible repair:** "Can we make time for this work? What should I put on hold?"
Use the second question only when a trade-off against current work is part of the task.

**Boundary:** If a manager explicitly asks for an action plan with assignments, keep
the supported assignments. If a contributor explicitly wants to raise missing
ownership, retain that concern as a concern or request, not as a completed decision.

## 4. Notes given a ceremonial header

**Context:** Private reminders for a familiar technical conversation, not a submitted
report or a formal record.

**Mismatch:** "Meeting / Duration / Prepared / Purpose", followed by a purpose
statement, executive summary, decision framework, and an authorization disclaimer.

**Possible repair:** A descriptive title and the actual topics or findings. Delete
unrequested administrative sections; do not replace each with a friendlier synonym.

**Boundary:** Required metadata in formal minutes, regulated records, or a supplied
template is not decoration. Preserve it. Blank fields are appropriate only when a
fillable record is requested, not as compensation for missing facts.

## 5. A proposal promoted into a promise

**Source:** "We could combine the jobs. No decision yet."

**Incorrect rewrite:** "We will combine the jobs next week."

**Repair:** "Combining the jobs is one option. We haven't decided yet."

Both the action status and lack of a date matter. Do not add an owner to make the
sentence more actionable. "Could" is meaningful uncertainty here, not empty hedging.

## 6. Criticism softened without permission

**Source position:** A vendor repeated findings already provided and still did not
answer the specific question. The author wants to keep that criticism.

**Incorrect rewrite:** "The response provides useful confirmation and a foundation
for further collaboration."

**Possible repair:** "The response mostly repeats what we had already sent and still
doesn't answer the question."

Do not erase the criticism to improve diplomacy or add insults to demonstrate
bluntness. Preserve its supported scope. A request to soften tone permits softer
wording, not a reversal of the assessment.

## 7. A technical manager treated as a nontechnical outsider

**Context:** The recipient works on the system and knows the named component.

**Mismatch:** Removing the component name and replacing a precise mechanism with
vague business language, followed by a basic explanation the reader does not need.

**Repair:** Retain the term and the finding. Explain only the genuinely new part.

**Boundary:** A recipient with the same job title but no project familiarity may need
background. Do not infer shared knowledge, or its absence, from seniority alone.

## 8. Source traceability turned into internal markup

**Context:** A short message to a colleague contains unexplained drafting markers
such as "[S1, §§3–14; S3, §§2–4]" copied from internal notes.

**Repair:** Replace them with the actual referenced document or link where needed
and available. Do not invent destinations or silently delete load-bearing attribution.
Keep a legitimate citation system in a research report that actually defines it.

Source identifiers are not evidence that the sources were read. Links are not
embedded originals; a catalogue is not a source archive. State that a file was
included only after inspecting the delivered artifact itself.

## 9. Corrective feedback treated as a formatting issue

**Feedback:** "That is not how I speak to my manager."

**Failed revision:** Reformat the table, shorten the headings, and retain questions
about organizational ownership and directives to assign work.

**Repair:** Revisit the author-recipient relationship and intended use. Rewrite the
misframed questions and inspect the rest of the draft for the same role. Preserve
unrelated accepted edits. An objection to role is not evidence of a narrow-screen issue.

A newer draft is not automatically better. If an attempted diagnosis is rejected,
stop repeating it. Do not call the next draft fixed solely because it is shorter.

## 10. Excessive correction in the opposite direction

- A scientist's passive sentence may be exact and conventional; leave it alone.
- A requested action log needs supported decisions, owners, and dates; do not make it conversational.
- A direct author can make a firm recommendation to a manager; do not turn it into timid permission-seeking.
- A supplied short message that already works needs no changes or invented findings.
- A creative passage may depend on repetition, metaphor, fragments, or irregular rhythm; preserve deliberate craft.
- A history or migration guide may describe changes; the objection to change-oriented durable reference prose does not apply automatically.

The rules protect the actual author and task. They do not mandate casualness,
shortness, deference, or one universally "human" style.
