# Voice calibration and optional profile

Match demonstrated writing habits without inventing a personality. The target or
authoritative source notes control facts; samples control supported style choices.
Use this procedure for drafting, rewriting, checking a voice match, or a requested
voice analysis. With no samples, retain the source's register and apply requested
tone without calling it an observed personal voice.

## Establish usable evidence

Use samples the user identifies as their writing or intended style. Prefer the same
language, channel, reader relationship, and purpose as the target. Separate quoted,
coauthored, and boilerplate passages. Do not infer a professional voice from rejected
model drafts, assistant messages, or the user's instructions to an assistant.

Use relevant samples together. One short message may establish direct wording, but
cannot establish a permanent ban on headings or a signature humor pattern. Different
contexts can support different voices. Do not demand a fixed sample count when a
conservative edit can proceed, or search unrelated private files to build a profile.

## Keep four kinds of information separate

| Status | Evidence | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| Observed | A traceable, preferably recurring feature in comparable samples | Match its function and approximate frequency where the genre fits |
| Requested | An explicit preference or accepted correction | Apply it within the current task without presenting it as a habitual trait |
| Tentative | A limited inference from sparse samples | Use cautiously for reversible surface choices; label it in a requested profile |
| Unsupported | An imagined belief, emotion, relationship, identity, experience, or habit | Do not apply it |

An explicit correction controls a surface preference for the task; meaning and
technical precision still constrain content. Samples containing mistakes do not
authorize introducing new errors.

## Observe and apply

Inspect sentence movement, paragraph openings, vocabulary, directness, uncertainty,
contractions, punctuation, transitions, greetings, and sign-offs. Record the actual
passage supporting an observation and its context. Counts are descriptive only;
report a frequency or average only after counting it.

Match broad tendencies without caricature. One fragment does not justify fragmenting
the whole document; one joke does not justify inventing more. Do not transfer project
facts, memorable sentences, opinions, or personal history from samples into the target.
Keep technical terminology consistent even when the author varies ordinary nouns.

“Professional” does not require institutional “we,” formal headings, or management
language. “Warm” can change wording without invented gratitude or intimacy. “Blunt”
keeps uncertainty and does not add insults. “Casual” does not require slang or typos.
Plain second-language English does not need idioms such as “move the needle.”

Compare the result with the target for facts and with comparable samples for style.
Check whether it became more formal, intimate, idiomatic, humorous, harsh, certain,
or authoritative than either source supports. Keep the referents of “I” and “we.”

A complaint such as “not how I talk to my manager” can reveal the wrong relationship
or artifact, not just the wrong punctuation. Revisit that context, fix all affected
passages, retain accepted edits, and stop repeating a rejected interpretation.

## Example calibration

Given two user-identified messages:

> Can we review the draft? I'd like to settle the wording first.

> Can we check the note? I don't think the last sentence helps.

| Status | Observation | Application | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observed | Both open with a collaborative question | Put an existing request early in a similar message | Do not invent a request in a status update |
| Observed | Both use first-person contractions | Use them where they fit the target | Do not invent first-person experience |
| Tentative | Neither has a heading | Prefer a plain paragraph for this short message | Two examples do not establish a global heading ban |
| Unsupported | The author jokes under pressure | None | No such evidence is supplied |

## Optional reusable profile

Use an existing authorized profile when relevant. Create or update a persistent
profile only when the user requests it. Copy the following fields into the selected
working/project file; do not edit the installed plugin copy. Retain general preferences
and minimal examples, not sensitive project facts or a reconstructed biography.

```markdown
# Voice profile

## Scope
- Language, channel, reader, relationship, and purpose:
- Relevant limits or contexts not represented:

## Samples
- Sample text or permitted reference:
- User-identified author/style source:
- Channel, recipient, and purpose:

## Observed habits
| Evidence | Habit | Applicable context | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|

## Explicit preferences and accepted corrections
- Preference, source of instruction, and scope:
- Greeting/sign-off, first-person use, terminology, length, formatting:
- Wording or structures the user explicitly rejected:

## Tentative preferences
- Inference, supporting sample, and uncertainty:
```

For voice analysis alone, return the requested profile with evidence and limits.
For a draft or rewrite, show it only when requested or when a material limitation
needs explaining. Do not describe a weakly evidenced match as verified.
